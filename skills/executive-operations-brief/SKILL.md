# Executive operations brief

Give leaders a decision-ready view of what changed, what is at risk, and what requires an owner.

## Define the management cadence

Agree on the audience, frequency, source systems, reporting timezone, data freshness threshold, and owners for sales, delivery, finance, customer support, and people operations. Start with data that can be verified; do not fabricate a company health score from incomplete signals.

## Compose the brief

Organize the brief around decisions, not data dumps:

1. Key changes since the prior brief.
2. Pipeline, revenue, cash, or collection risks.
3. Delivery milestones, blocked work, and capacity risks.
4. Customer escalations and recurring support issues.
5. Decisions required, each with a proposed owner and deadline.
6. Data gaps and confidence notes.

Link every important claim to the source system or label it an estimate. Keep facts, interpretation, and recommendation separate.

## Operate safely

The brief may create draft tasks or reminders. Require approval before sending executive communications, assigning work, changing forecasts, publishing data, or making commitments. If sources disagree, report the discrepancy rather than selecting a convenient value.

## Improve the brief

At the end of each cycle, record which decisions were acted upon and which sections were ignored. Remove vanity metrics and add only measures that changed a decision or prevented a surprise.
